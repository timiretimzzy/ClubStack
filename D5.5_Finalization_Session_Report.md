## 4. Specification basis and the decision where the specification is silent

The Phase 2, 3, 7, 8, and 9 specifications were re-read. **All of them are silent** on how to
reconstruct tax for a refund, and the specifications explicitly forbid inventing financial business
rules.

The directive resolves this silence directly, and it is consistent with the carried decision that
ClubStack's monetary and tax values are authoritative: use the original refunded line's treatment and
rate wherever the sale-line data exists, rather than deriving a rate ClubStack never charged. That
decision is implemented as specified in section 5. No business rule was invented: every value in a
credit note traces to a value on the original sale line.

## 5. Refund tax design, the fallback, and inconsistent-data behaviour

**Design — authoritative path.** `SaleRefundLineDto` now carries `TaxableBase`, `TaxAmount`,
`TaxTreatment`, `TaxRateName`, and `TaxRate`. `EntityFrameworkSaleService.RefundAsync` copies these
from the original sale line at refund time. `FiscalDocumentBuilder` then, for each refunded line,
emits the original treatment, maps it to the corresponding device tax ID, and credits that line's
own tax. The document's tax total is the sum of the per-line tax, so a full refund reverses the
invoice tax exactly, bracket by bracket.

**Why the previous approach was wrong.** The credit note previously took a proportional share of the
invoice's tax total. For a mixed-rate invoice this is arithmetically incorrect: the invoice's tax
belongs entirely to the standard-rated line, so smearing it across the lines credits the zero-rated
line with tax it never paid. `[TEST-SEAM]` `Mixed_rate_credit_note_credits_each_line_into_its_own_tax_bracket`
pins this case: a standard-rated and a zero-rated line produce a credit note where the zero-rated
bracket receives zero tax.

**Fallback for historical data.** Where an existing refund snapshot has no per-line tax data, the
previous proportional behaviour is retained as a clearly-labelled fallback so historical rows remain
re-readable and refundable. `[TEST-SEAM]` `Legacy_refund_snapshot_without_tax_details_still_credits_the_invoice_total`
covers it. The fallback is deliberately not applied to new refunds, which always snapshot per-line tax.

**Inconsistent data.** Where only some lines carry tax data, no reconciliation is invented.
`FiscalDocumentBuilder` throws `FiscalDocumentBuildException`, and the processor persists the
document as `Failed` with an operator-actionable reason rather than guessing.
`[TEST-SEAM]` `Inconsistent_refund_tax_snapshot_fails_the_document_with_a_reason`.

## 6. Device tax rate mapping and the resolver defect corrected

`[INSPECTION]` The real configuration is the defect's natural habitat: it declares a bracket named
`Zero Rated` at `0.155` with every other bracket at `0`, so a "highest positive rate" selection picks
the zero-rated bracket for a document that requires standard-rated tax.

`TaxIds.ResolveStandardRated` previously selected the highest positive rate, which could select a
bracket whose own name indicates zero-rated or exempt. It now excludes such brackets, keeps the
existing highest-positive-rate behaviour for genuine candidates, and defers genuine ties to the
engine rather than picking arbitrarily.

The defect was corrected **in ClubStack's resolution logic**, as the directive requires. No rate was
misrepresented to the device to work around it, and the real configuration was not modified.

`[TEST-SEAM]` Five new tests in `FiscalisationTaxIdTests` prove a zero-rated or exempt bracket cannot
be selected when standard-rated tax is required, and that ties defer to the engine.

## 7. Refund integrity design and enforcement location

Enforced in the **database**, not only in application code:

- `FiscalisationEntity.Refund` navigation and a `RefundId → sale_refunds.Id` foreign key with
  `DeleteBehavior.Restrict`, so a refund cannot be deleted out from under its credit note.
- A **filtered unique index** on non-null `RefundId`, so one refund can produce at most one credit
  note. A second document for the same refund is rejected by the database itself.

Migration `20260926054103_D55Finalization_RefundIntegrity` applies both. The startup migration
check was updated from 9 to 10 expected migrations and is asserted by a test.

`[TEST-SEAM]` One refund produces exactly one fiscal document; a duplicate for the same refund is
rejected; an orphan `RefundId` is rejected; a normal invoice coexists unaffected; two independent
refunds each get their own document.

## 8. Recovery of failed fiscalisation, including the receipt-holding case

`[TEST-SEAM]` A rejection that still returns a device receipt is re-drivable. The device can return a
receipt global number and a rejection in the same response, so a naive "does it have a receipt?"
test would strand such a document permanently. The retry path re-drives the device for `Failed`
records regardless of receipt presence, so an authorised operator can always recover a failure.

**Bug found and fixed in this session — a document could be reported fiscalised without a fiscal
result.** The retry path had a shortcut: for a record in `PendingRetry` or `Submitted`, or with
`PendingSync` set, it flushed the engine's offline queue and, if the queue reported `0` remaining,
marked the document `Fiscalised`. But `DeferAsync` persists `PendingRetry` **with no receipt at all**
when the device throws before fiscalising. So a document the device never accepted could reach
`Fiscalised` on the strength of an unrelated document having drained from the queue. That is exactly
the outcome the directive forbids: no sale may silently appear fiscalised without a fiscal result.

`EngineHoldsDocument` is now gated on positive evidence that the device actually fiscalised the
document — a receipt global number on the record. Without a receipt the engine's queue says nothing
about *this* document, so the document is re-driven through the device instead. Two regression tests
pin the behaviour:

- `Retrying_a_document_the_device_never_accepted_does_not_rely_on_an_empty_queue` — the device throws,
  the record defers with no receipt, the queue is empty, the device recovers; the document becomes
  `Fiscalised` only because the device was actually driven, and it holds a real receipt.
- `A_deferred_document_with_an_empty_queue_is_never_marked_fiscalised_without_a_receipt` — the device
  keeps refusing; the record stays visibly unresolved rather than drifting to `Fiscalised`.

The legitimate reconciliation path is preserved:
`Retrying_an_offline_accepted_document_flushes_the_engine_queue` still passes, so a document the
engine genuinely holds is reconciled through the queue rather than transacted twice.

`[INSPECTION]` A related crash window was checked and is safe: `Submitted` is assigned in memory
before the device call but is not persisted until after the result is applied, so a crash before the
device call cannot leave a persisted `Submitted` record.

## 9. Duplicate avoidance and idempotency

- **Already accepted by the engine** → reconciled through the engine queue, never transacted twice
  (section 8).
- **Same refund twice** → rejected by the unique index (section 7).
- **Already `Fiscalised`** → retry is a no-op, returning the document unchanged.
- **Repeated worker or repeated retry** → safe; tests assert the record and its identifiers do not
  change on a second pass.

## 11. Test seam and the determinism of the proof

`[TEST-SEAM]` `IFiscalisationGateway` is the seam. `FakeFiscalisationGateway` supports per-test
control of acceptance, rejection message, thrown exceptions, pending queue count, and call counters,
so failure, retry, duplicate, and idempotency cases are driven deterministically rather than by
timing or sleeps.

Counters are scoped per document (`InvoiceNumber`) rather than globally, because the outbox drains a
shared database and global counters produced order-dependent failures between tests. This fixed two
pre-existing order dependencies (`Search_filters_by_status_and_sale` and two retry call-count tests).

## 19. Security posture, including anything deliberately not persisted or logged

- **No credential was printed, copied, logged, or moved** during the configuration review. The
  licence key, activation key, and any certificate private material were not reproduced in this
  report, in logs, in audit rows, or in test output.
- **Activation and licence keys are write-only.** They are passed straight through to the engine's
  own licence-enforcing entry point and are never stored by ClubStack. The server DTO has no key
  field, so no key can be returned to the browser.
- **No secret is rendered in the SPA**, enforced structurally by the DTO shape.
- **Audit logging records actions, not secrets.** Device-management attempts record the action, the
  actor, and the outcome; they never record key material.
- **Certificate private material is never read by ClubStack**; only the folder path is displayed, and
  only to operators holding device-view permission.
- `C:\ProgramData\FiscalStackZW` was read but not modified, preserving active development state.

## 20. Known limitations and open risks

1. **Live FDMS is entirely unverified** (section 15). This is the dominant open risk.
2. **The historical proportional tax fallback remains** for pre-existing refund snapshots. It is
   arithmetically wrong for mixed-rate historical invoices, and is retained deliberately so those
   rows stay refundable. Any such historical refund should be checked manually.
3. **The real device's tax table is incoherent** and was not modified. Until it is corrected on the
   device, live bracket IDs will contradict bracket names regardless of ClubStack's resolver.
4. **A real network timeout after the device accepted a document cannot be reproduced safely.** The
   receipt-gated reconciliation in section 8 is the mitigation, verified deterministically, but the
   real timing case remains unproven live.
5. **The crash window between device acceptance and result persistence is not eliminable** without
   device-side idempotency. The design prefers reconciling through the engine queue over
   re-transacting, and refuses to infer success from an empty queue.
6. **The API authorisation suite uses a stub service**, so it proves authorisation and routing, not
   end-to-end persistence through a live fiscalisation.
7. **No attached Fiscal Device Gateway API Specification v7.2** was available, so the adapter is
   built against the DLL's observed surface rather than a vendor document.
8. **SPA has no automated UI tests**; Part 10 findings are `[INSPECTION]` against the source and a
   clean strict build, not browser-executed assertions.
